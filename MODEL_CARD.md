# Model card: wrist-sensor-robustness activity classifier + window validator

## Model details

- **Served model:** XGBoost gradient-boosted trees on 71 hand-crafted features of a 10 s, 20 Hz, 3-axis smartwatch accelerometer window. 300 trees, depth 6, learning rate 0.1, subsample/colsample 0.8, seed 42.
- **Comparison model (not served):** 3-block 1D CNN implemented from scratch in NumPy, 19,538 parameters.
- **Gate:** `WindowValidator` — rule checks (flatline, gap, clipping, motion-conditioned noise, magnitude) plus an IsolationForest anomaly score calibrated to a tail probability on held-out training subjects. Flags at tail probability < 0.01 or any rule failure; flagged windows are not passed to the model by the API.
- **Version:** 0.1.0. Portfolio/research artifact; not affiliated with any company.

## Intended use

- Demonstrating and measuring how activity recognition from a wrist accelerometer degrades under realistic sensor faults, and how a pre-model data-quality gate trades coverage for reliability.
- Educational/reference implementation of subject-wise evaluation and data-quality validation for wearable time series.

**Out of scope:** clinical, diagnostic, insurance, workplace-monitoring, or safety-critical decisions; any population or device not resembling the training data; free-living deployment without fresh validation.

## Training data

WISDM Smartphone and Smartwatch Activity and Biometrics Dataset (Weiss, 2019; UCI id 507; DOI 10.24432/C5HK59; CC BY 4.0). Smartwatch accelerometer only. 51 adult volunteers, 18 scripted activities of ~3 min each, lab setting, one watch model, one wrist. Preprocessing: drop malformed/duplicate samples, split recordings at gaps > 250 ms, resample each segment to exactly 20 Hz, 10 s windows with 50% overlap → 31,014 windows. The served model is fit on all 51 subjects.

## Evaluation

All numbers are from `results/tables/` (generated by the code; see README).

- **Protocol:** 5-fold cross-validation grouped by subject (10–11 unseen people per fold); mean ± std over folds.
- **Clean, subject-wise:** GBM accuracy 75.2 ± 4.7%, macro-F1 75.7 ± 4.2%; CNN accuracy 67.3 ± 5.4%, macro-F1 67.6 ± 5.1%. Per-fold GBM accuracy ranges from 70.4% to 82.7%, so expect large person-to-person variation.
- **Leakage check:** a random-window split (same people and overlapping windows on both sides) reports 93.6% GBM accuracy (+18.3 points) and 83.2% CNN accuracy (+15.9 points). That number is not a valid performance estimate.
- **Robustness** (macro-F1, GBM, from 75.5% clean on the robustness sample): noise σ = 1 m/s² → 49.6%; 45° orientation rotation → 59.6%; swapped x/y axes → 50.8%; 30% sample dropout → 65.8%; clipping at ±8 m/s² → 44.4%; clock running 20% fast → 71.5%. Full tables in the README.
- **Validator:** ~1.4% false rejections on clean windows from unseen subjects; detects 100% of dropout, 85–100% of clipping at ±8 m/s² or tighter, 7–69% of noise at σ = 0.5–4 m/s², and ≤ 4% of rotations, axis swaps and rate drift.
- **Gating** on a stream of 70% clean / 30% corrupted windows: GBM macro-F1 69.6% ungated → 72.5% with validator gating (11.2% of windows rejected) → 74.3% rejecting the same number of lowest-confidence windows instead.

The CNN and the per-fold metrics are reported with the same protocol in the README.

## Known limitations and risks

- **Silent failure under orientation change.** Rotating the watch on the wrist or wearing it on the other arm produces plausible-looking windows the validator cannot flag, while accuracy drops sharply (and much more for the CNN). A passing quality score does not mean the prediction is trustworthy.
- **Moderate noise is only partly detected** (≤ 29% at σ ≤ 2 m/s²), yet costs 11–42 macro-F1 points.
- **Lab-to-field gap.** Scripted activities, one device, 51 volunteers. Activity boundaries, free-living behaviour, other devices and other populations are untested.
- **Confusable activities.** The eating activities (soup, chips, pasta, sandwich) and other low-motion hand activities are intrinsically hard to separate from a wrist accelerometer.
- **Simulated corruptions** are simplified models of real faults; the 70/30 mixed-stream composition is an assumption.
- **Calibration of the model's confidence under shift was not separately evaluated**; confidence-based gating worked well on this stream but is not guaranteed to on other shifts.

## Ethical considerations

Activity recognition from wearables can be used for surveillance (e.g. of workers or patients). This project is intended only for studying robustness. The data comes from consenting volunteers in a published research dataset and is used under its CC BY 4.0 license with attribution; no new personal data was collected.

`make data baseline robustness analyze train-final test` — all randomness seeded with 42 and derived per-fold seeds; pinned versions in `requirements.txt`.
